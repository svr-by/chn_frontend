# CHN — Procurement & Logistics Platform

A plain-language overview of the CHN application for users, stakeholders, and presentations.

---

## What is CHN?

**CHN** is a unified workspace for companies that procure materials, work with suppliers, pay invoices, and organize deliveries.

Instead of scattered spreadsheets, email threads, and chat messages, everyone involved works in one place — from the first purchase request to goods arrival.

---

## Who is it for?

The system is built for **B2B companies**: manufacturers, trading organizations, distributors, and any business with a buyer–supplier–payment–shipment chain.

The same company can act as:

- **Buyer** — when procuring materials for itself
- **Supplier** — when responding to client requests
- **Both** — e.g. fulfilling a client order while sourcing components from its own suppliers

Real supply chains work this way. CHN supports it without splitting users into separate “buyer portal” and “supplier portal” experiences.

## How it works: request to delivery

A typical end-to-end scenario:

### 1. Setup

A company registers, invites colleagues, and assigns roles: procurement, logistics, accounting, and so on.

In parallel, the company establishes **partner links** with other organizations — suppliers and clients.

### 2. Material request

Procurement creates a **request** — a list of needed materials with quantities and deadlines.

Requests can be entered manually or **imported from a file** (e.g. CSV) when there are many line items.

### 3. Request for quotation

The request is sent to one or more partner suppliers.

Each supplier sees the inbound request and prepares a **quote** with prices and terms.

### 4. Selection

The buyer compares all quotes and **confirms a selection** — different line items can come from different suppliers with chosen quantities.

### 5. Invoice and payment

The supplier whose lines were selected issues an **invoice**.

The buyer registers a **payment** and can attach proof. Accounting can review and confirm the payment.

### 6. Shipment

The supplier creates a **shipping invoice** — what was sent, in what quantity, linked to the invoice.

### 7. Consolidation and delivery

The buyer groups several shipments into a **consolidation** — a single delivery for transport (road, air, rail, sea).

This simplifies logistics planning and tracking.

## Roles within a company

Each employee has a role with a permission set. The company owner can fine-tune access per person.

| Role | Focus |
|------|-------|
| **Owner** | Company management, members, permissions |
| **Admin** | Broad operational access |
| **Procurement** | Requests, quotes, selections, invoices |
| **Logistics** | Shipments and consolidations |
| **Accountant** | Payments and confirmation |
| **Warehouse** | Receiving and warehouse ops (planned) |
| **Viewer** | Read-only access |

One user can belong to multiple companies and switch between them.

## Core capabilities

### Partner network

Catalog of linked companies: invite partners, manage inbound and outbound links. Procurement documents flow only between established partners.

### Procurement

Material requests, multi-supplier quote collection, comparison, and line-level selection.

### Finance

Supplier invoices, payment registration, proof uploads, payment confirmation.

### Logistics

Shipping invoices, consolidation of shipments, transport mode selection.

### Collaboration

Document comments and event notifications — conversation stays attached to the request, invoice, or shipment instead of getting lost in email.

### Traceability

The platform’s core value: **any line item can be traced across the full chain**.

Examples:

- Which request produced this shipment line?
- Which quote was selected for this invoice line?
- Which consolidation includes this delivery?

Each line keeps a link to the previous document — from the first request line to final delivery.

### Integrations

- Import requests from files
- Export data and connect to ERP / warehouse systems

## Key benefits

1. **One process, one system** — procurement, finance, and logistics stay connected.
2. **Transparency** — buyer and supplier see current document status; no black boxes in email.
3. **Flexible roles** — each company defines who can do what.
4. **Real supply chains** — buy and sell on the same platform.
5. **Control and audit** — activity history and lineage simplify disputes and internal control.

## One-paragraph summary

**CHN** is a platform where companies run procurement from the first request to goods arrival: request quotes from partners, select the best terms, pay invoices, issue shipments, and plan delivery. All documents are linked, employees work within their roles, and any line item can be traced from request to warehouse.
